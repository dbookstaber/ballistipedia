# CEP literature

The literature on the [circular error probable (CEP)](circular-error-probable.md) is extensive and diverse: Applications for CEP are found in areas such as target shooting, missile ballistics, error analysis for geopositional asessments, or positional accuracy of navigation and guidance systems like GPS. The statistical foundations in quadratic forms of normal variables are important for analyzing the power of inference tests. The Hoyt and Rayleigh distribution have applications in (wireless) signal processing.

# Groups of CEP publications

The following list is by no means intended to be complete. Beware that the quality of the listed publications is not uniformly high. The relevant publications may be roughly categorized into different groups:

- Articles that develop a CEP estimator or the modification of one – e. g., RAND-234 ([RAND Corporation, 1952](#rand1952)), modified RAND-234 ([Pesapane & Irvine, 1977](#pesapane1977)), [Grubbs (1964)](#grubbs1964), Rayleigh ([Culpepper, 1978](#culpepper1978); [Saxena & Singh, 2005](#saxena2005); [Singh, 1992](#singh1992)), [Ethridge (1983](#ethridge1983); [Hogg, 1967](#hogg1967)), [Bell (1973)](#bell1973), [Nicholson (1974)](#nicholson1974), [Siouris (1993)](#siouris1993), [Krempasky (2003)](#krempasky2003), [Ignani (2010)](#ignani2010), general bivariate normal ([DiDonato & Jarnagin, 1961a](#didonato1961a); [Evans, Govindarajulu, & Barthoulot, 1985](#evans1985)), and Bayes ([Spall & Maryak, 1992](#spall1992)).
- Some articles focus on the confidence intervals for CEP ([DiDonato, 2007](#didonato2007); [Sathe, Joshi, & Nabar, 1991](#sathe1991); [Taub & Thomas, 1983b](#taub1983b); [Thomas et al., 1973a](#thomas1973a); [Thomas & Crigler, 1974](#thomas1974); [Zhang & An, 2012](#zhang2012)).
- <a id="compstudies"></a>Articles or Master's theses comparing the characteristics of CEP estimators in different scenarios ([Blischke & Halpin, 1966](#blischke1966); [Elder, 1986](#elder1986); [Kamat, 1962](#kamat1962); [McMillan & McMillan, 2008](#mcmillan2008); [Moranda, 1959](#moranda1959), [1960](#moranda1960); [Nelson, 1988](#nelson1988); [Puhek, 1992](#puhek1992); [Tongue, 1993](#tongue1993); [Taub & Thomas, 1983a](#taub1983a); [Wang, Jia, Yang, & Wang, 2013](#wang2013a); [Wang, Yang, Jia, & Wang, 2013](#wang2013b); [Wang, Yang, Yan, Wang, & Song, 2014](#wang2014); [Williams, 1997](#williams1997)).
- Publications studying the correlated bivariate normal distribution with 0 mean, re-written in polar coordinates radius and angle ([Chew & Boyce, 1962](#chew1962); [Greenwalt & Shultz, 1962](#greenwalt1962); [Harter, 1960](#harter1960); [Hoover, 1984](#hoover1984); [Hoyt, 1947](#hoyt1947)). The distribution of the radius is known as the Hoyt ([1947](#hoyt1947)) distribution. The closed form expression for its cumulative distribution function has only recently been identified as the symmetric difference between two Marcum Q-functions ([Marcum, 1950](#marcum1950); [Paris, 2009](#paris2009)). The latter is the complement (with respect to unity) of a special case of the non-central $\chi^2$-distribution ([Nuttall, 1975](#nuttall1975)). A nearly correct closed-form solution for the 50% quantile is given by [Krempasky (2003)](#krempasky2003), [Childs (1975)](#childs1975) and [Ignani (2010)](#ignani2010) provide polynomial approximations for the 50%; 90%, 95%, and 99% quantile. The statistical literature on coverage problems in the multivariate normal distribution is reviewed in [Guenther & Terragno (1964)](#guenther1964). From the perspective of radio engineering, Beckmann ([1962](#beckmann1962), [1964](#beckmann1964)) works out the relationship between the Rayleigh, Rice and Hoyt distribution and their generalization.
- [Moranda (1960)](#moranda1960), DiDonato and Jarnagin ([1961a](#didonato1961a), [1961b](#didonato1961b), [1962a](#didonato1962a), [1962b](#didonato1962b)) Gilliland ([1962](#gilliland1962), [1974](#gilliland1974)), [Cadwell (1964)](#cadwell1964) as well as [Shultz (1963)](#shultz1963), [Bell (1973)](#bell1973) and [Evans et al. (1985)](#evans1985) develop methods to use the correlated bivariate normal distribution for CEP estimation when systematic accuracy bias must be taken into account. This requires integrating the distribution over a disc that is not centered on the true mean of the shot group but on the point of aim. This so-called offset circle probability is the probability of a quadratic form of a normal variable The exact distribution of quadratic forms is a weighted average of non-central $\chi^{2}$-distributions and difficult to calculate without numerical tools. Therefore, the [Patnaik (1949)](#patnaik1949) two-moment central $\chi^{2}$-approximation or the Pearson ([Imhof, 1961](#imhof1961); [Pearson, 1959](#pearson1959)) three-moment central $\chi^{2}$-approximation are often used. Recently, [Liu, Tang & Zhang (2009)](#liu2009) proposed a four-moment non-central $\chi^{2}$-approximation.
- <a id="algos"></a>A number of articles present algorithms for the efficient numerical calculation of the Hoyt cumulative distribution function (cdf), as well as for its inverse, the quantile function (DiDonato, [2004](#didonato2004), [2007](#didonato2007); [Gillis, 1991](#gillis1991);  [Govindarajulu 1986](#govindarajulu1986); [Pyati, 1993](#pyati1993); [Rogers, 1993](#rogers1993); [Shnidman, 1995](#shnidman1995)). The distribution of quadratic forms of normal random variables and numerical algorithms for their calculation were studied by [Cacoullos (1984)](#cacoullos1984), [Davies (1980)](#davies1980), Farebrother ([1984](#farebrother1984), [1990](#farebrother1990)), [Imhof (1961)](#imhof1961), [Sheil & O'Muircheartaigh (1977)](#sheil1977), and [Kuonen (1999)](#kuonen1999). A comparison and implementation numerical integration algorithms can be found in [Duchesne and Lafaye de Micheaux (2010)](#duchesne2010).
- [Childs (1975)](#childs1975), [Ignani (2010)](#ignani2010), Singh ([1962](#singh1962), [1970](#singh1970)),  [DiDonato (1988)](#didonato1988), [Siouris (1993)](#siouris1993) and [Thomas et al. (1973b)](#thomas1973b), treat the Spherical Error Probable SEP.
- <a id="gps"></a>The GPS-related literature on accuracy measures in navigation includes [Mertikas (1985)](#mertikas1985) and [Chin (1987)](#chin1987).
